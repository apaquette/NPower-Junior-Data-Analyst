# Framework and Tools

## Introduction
- one of the most valuable skills you can offer
- not limited to managers or team leaders
- helps you navigate your responsibilities, contribute meaningfully
## What is a Project?
- a temporary and unique endeavour with a beginning and end
- defined by three characteristics
	- Goal-oriented
	- Temporary
	- Unique
## What is Management?
- Management: science of ensuring scheduled activities goes according to plan
- Key elements: 
	- planning: roadmap that outlines a project's goals
	- organising: structuring and arranging resources
	- directing: providing leadership and guidance to the team
	- controlling: monitoring and adjusting actives to ensure they align with the plan
## Why do we need Project Management
- essential for achieving organisational goals
- provide structure, coordination, oversight
- ensure efficient use of resources
## Constraints of Project Management
- Cost
- Time
- Scope
## Steps
- Initiation
	- Create a project plan with tasks, timelines, and resources
	- Define roles and responsibilities
	- Develop a budget
	- Identify risks and make a risk management plan
- Execution
	- write and compile content
	- Design the layout
	- review and revise
	- Ensure it aligns with the target audience
- Monitor and Control
	- Track the status of each task
	- Address issues or challenges
	- Ensure the project is within budget and timeline
- Closing
	- Conduct final review
	- Launch product
	- Communication completion with stakeholders
	- Gather feedback for improvement

## Frameworks
- **Waterfall**: sequential project framework where tasks progress through distinct phases
- **Agile**: flexible and iterative approach, emphasising collaboration, adaptability, and customer feedback
- **Lean**: focused on minimising waste and maximising value by continuously improving efficiency
## Finding the Right Fit
- there is no on-size-fits-all framework
- no single framework will work for every project or team
- the "right" framework means choosing the one that's right for your organisational needs
- plan-driven (waterfall), change-driven (agile), process-driven (lean)
## Waterfall
- traditional and linear method
- original project management frameworks
- 5 steps
	- Plan: requirements are gathered
	- Build: actual development
	- Test
	- Review: stakeholders assess the product and provide feedback
	- Deploy: Final approved product is released
- Pros
	- well-defined phases
	- easy to understand and implement
	- client involvement primarily occurs during requirements gathering
- Cons
	- Inflexible to changes
	- May not accommodate evolving requirements
	- Testing occurs towards the end of the project
	- Entire project must be completed before delivering functional components
	- Limited customer interactions until deployment
- Where Waterfall is suitable
	- Construction Projects
	- Manufacturing processes
	- Regulatory Compliance
	- Simple Software Projects
## Agile
- iterative and collaborative approach
- focuses on flexibility, adaptability, and continuous improvement
- allows for incremental development of a project
- involve a series of SPRINTS
	- a miniature project with its own design, development, and testing
	- duration: typically two to four weeks
- Sprint Planning
	- team conducts a planning meeting
	- product owner presents prioritised items
	- team collaboratively selects items
- Daily Stand-ups
	- team holds daily meetings
	- members share progress, and plan next steps
	- meetings are brief and ensure continuous communication
- Development
	- team works on backlog items
	- goal is to produce potentially shippable product increment
- Sprint Review
	- team showcases completed work to stakeholders
	- feedback is gathered
- Sprint Retrospective
	- team reflects on the Sprint, discussing what went well, what could be improved, and identifying action items for the next sprint
- Where agile is suitable
	- Software Development
	- Information Technology
	- Marketing
	- Product Development
	- Retail and E-commerce
- Agile umbrella
	- Scrum
	- SAFE
	- DSDM
	- Crystal
	- XP
	- Kanban
	- Lean
	- FDD

## Scrum Roles
- Product Owner
	- responsible for defining the product backlog
	- represent the voice of the customer
	- make decisions about what needs to be developed next
	- collaborates with stakeholders
- Scrum Master
	- expert on the scrum framework
	- only one scrum master per team
	- responsible for overseeing the scrum task board
	- helps the rest of the team understand and apply scrum practices
	- remove impediments and obstacles that hinder progress
	- fosters a culture of continuous improvement
- Development Team
	- responsible for executing tasks and achieving goals
	- consists of professionals responsible for delivering the product
	- are cross-functional and self-organising
	- collaborate closely and are accountable for delivering high-quality products

## Key Scrum Meetings
- Daily Scrum
- Sprint Review
- Sprint Retrospective
- Sprint Planning

## What Scrum is NOT:
- not a status or recording meeting
- not for micromanagement
- not only for the Scrum master
- not a planning meeting
- not a technical discussion
- must consist of three questions
	- what did you do yesterday?
	- what are you doing today?
	- are you facing any impediments?
## What is a workplace impediment
- anything that slows down or diminishes the pace of the team
- typically considered an obstacle or challenge beyond the control of the team
- can take various forms
	- external dependencies
	- technical challenges
	- lack of resources
	- organisational issues
